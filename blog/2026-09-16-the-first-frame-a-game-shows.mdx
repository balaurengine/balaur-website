A game's first frames are the ones with nothing to draw: the scene is being
built, the textures are being uploaded, and the window is already open.
`[application] splash` is the picture that goes there.

{/* truncate */}

![The editor's own splash: the logo centred on black with a loading bar under it](/img/blog/editor_splash.webp)

Two lines in `project.toml` are the whole of it. The picture is
project-relative, fits itself to the screen and keeps its aspect, and shows on
every target the engine builds for, a browser tab included.

```toml
[application]
splash = "art/logo.png"
splash_seconds = 1.5
```

A load that takes longer than its seconds holds the splash. There is no
setting for that. A run that reports a load is held until it says it is
through, and a run that reports nothing goes when its seconds are up.

```rune
ui::set_loading(0.4, "textures");
// ...
ui::loaded();
```

- `set_loading` takes a fraction and a label, and draws a bar under the
  picture in the theme's accent colour.
- `loaded` says the load is through. The splash goes once the seconds have
  also passed, so a fast machine still shows the logo rather than flashing it.
- The editor drives its own with these: the first pass places the docks, the
  second fills the tree, and the third is the first frame with everything in
  place.
- The seconds are engine time, so a recorded session replays the splash for
  exactly as long as the run showed it.

The manual has it under [Shipping](/docs/manual/shipping#the-splash).
