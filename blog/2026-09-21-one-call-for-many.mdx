A scene of five thousand sprites submitted five thousand draws, and the GPU sat idle while the CPU wrote them. It submits one now.

{/* truncate */}

![Sixteen sprites cut from one sheet, turned, scaled, tinted and flipped apart, drawn in a single call](/img/manual/batching_sprite.webp)

## In two dimensions

- Sprites and shapes sharing a mesh, an image and a material draw in one call. The run is cut along the draw order, so what covers what does not change.
- A sheet frame joins the run. The frame is a rectangle carried by the instance, where it used to be a private copy of the built-in quad with rewritten coordinates.
- Five thousand circles offscreen at 1600x1000 went from 22.7 ms of render CPU to 0.41.

## In three dimensions

![Eighteen cubes turned, scaled and tinted apart, lit and casting shadows, drawn in a single call](/img/manual/batching_mesh.webp)

- Meshes group by geometry, texture and material. Geometry is depth-tested, so a group needs no place in an order and nodes that draw alike join one wherever they sit in the tree.
- Two thousand cuboids went from 30.7 ms of render CPU to 0.77. Two thousand balls went from 31.9 to 1.49, and from 13.5 ms on the GPU to 5.50.

## What keeps a draw of its own

- A polygon, a polyline, a node with its own material, a skinned mesh, a morphed one, a cloner and a model with levels of detail.
- A mesh scaled unevenly, because the instance's matrix goes through its normals and only an even scale comes out of that unchanged.

Nothing turns this on. There is no component and no setting.
