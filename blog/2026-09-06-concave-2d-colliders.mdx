Split a concave polygon into multiple polygons that overlap for better physics results and stability

<Clip name="concave_beam" alt="Two tables with a beam spawned inside each, across a seam. In the plain one the beam stays wedged; in the grown one it is pushed up and clear of the top within a second" />

Cutting a polygon used to leave seams, where small objects could get stuck. Erin Catto's
[Stuck Inside](https://box2d.org/posts/2020/04/stuck-inside/) article explains about
overlaps the pieces by hand. This does it automatically.

`overlap` is configurable, 0.9 by default (it leaves a bit of empty room). At 0 you get the plain cut.

![One shape at four overlap settings, and three stars showing how many pieces pile onto one point](/img/blog/concave_dial_stack.webp)

## Performance

What the times look like (at build time):

| kind | ns | result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| trimesh | 118 | still inside |
| convex_decomposition, overlap 0 | 187 | still inside |
| convex_decomposition, overlap 0.9 | 367 | out |

- A trimesh is the cheaper static collider when bodies only touch it from
  outside. This kind is for dynamic bodies.

## Also

- Mass comes from the ungrown pieces, so a table weighs what its area says.
- A solids-mode Godot `CollisionPolygon2D` imports as this kind.
- `geometry2d.convex_decomposition` hands the pieces to a script.
