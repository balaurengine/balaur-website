A label is a picture of its text no longer. Marks in it can be clicked and
hovered, a drag selects it, and a number carries units.

{/* truncate */}

![A label with a link and a hint, a selectable line, and two numbers with arrows and units](/img/blog/label_links_and_numbers.webp)

## What landed

- **`[url=target]`.** The span wears the theme's link colour and an
  underline, the pointer over it is a hand, and a click calls `on_link` with
  the target. What to do with it is the game's: a scene, a shop, a browser.
- **`[hint=text]`.** The same span, saying something while the pointer rests
  on it. The hover text belongs to the span, not to the whole label.
- **`selectable`.** A drag selects, the platform's copy key takes it, double
  click takes a word and treble takes the block. A seed or an error code is
  now something a player can send you.
- **`arrows` and `suffix`.** A `drag_value` draws a step up and a step down
  inside its own box, each moving by `step` within `min` and `max`, and the
  units follow the number: `12 px`, `4.5 m/s`.

Labels are still shaped by the engine's own text stack. That is what keeps
right-to-left runs in order, holds one glyph atlas behind the HUD and the
world, and measures a width a headless run agrees with.
