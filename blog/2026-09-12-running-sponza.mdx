- A glTF material arrives with every factor and map, the alpha mode and its
  cutoff, the index of refraction and the volume. Each material is one mesh
  node, so Sponza is 25 nodes over 103 primitives.
- The import writes the file's own wrapping beside each image, so the marble
  floor arrives tiled. Clamped, a floor whose coordinates run past one draws as
  a single stretched texel.

## What a surface reads

`package::mesh` binds the sky as an environment map, the occlusion the prepass
measured, the reflection probes, and the scene that glass refracts. Each has a
one-pixel stand-in, so nothing branches on absence.

![A chrome ball, a pane of glass and a mirrored floor under a sky](/img/blog/3d_look_surfaces.webp)

- Image-based lighting takes the place of `environment.ambient` rather than
  adding to it, since both stand for the same bounced light.
- Glass is `[surface]` on the material: `transmission`, `ior`, `thickness` and
  a volume colour. It draws after the opaque scene resolves, refracts it, and
  keeps its own highlights on top.
- A mirror renders the scene from its own plane, in place of what the sky
  would show.
- A `reflection_probe` is a box a room was captured inside. A surface in it
  reflects the room, aimed at the box rather than at infinity.

## Occlusion, and the pass it needed

![Two boxes on a floor, without occlusion and with it](/img/blog/3d_look_occlusion.webp)

Every 3D material draws a geometry prepass, a project's own included. That
prepass feeds the buffer occlusion reads, so every surface darkens in its
creases.

## Four passes to finish on

- `vignette`, `aberration`, `grain` and `pixelate` are names on `camera.post`,
  drawn where the list puts them. `fxaa` and `sharpen` join them from the
  renderer.
- The layer stack ships in the editor's library: colour, image, gradient,
  noise, matcap, fresnel, toon and outline, each a flag with its own knobs.
